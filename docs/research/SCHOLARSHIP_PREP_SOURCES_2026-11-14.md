## A. When to start and grade-by-grade timeline (all federal guidance, undated, verified)
1. Grade 9: start a running list of awards, honors, paid and volunteer work and activities, and update it all through high school. Source: FSA, 9th Grade College Prep Checklists, https://studentaid.gov/resources/prepare-for-college/checklists/9th-grade. Quote: "Start a list of your awards, honors, paid and volunteer work" ... Federal guidance. Verified.
2. Grade 9: pick activities for quality, not quantity. Same page. Quote: "it's quality (not quantity) that counts." Verified.
3. Grade 10: learn the difference between grants, loans, work-study and scholarships; meet your counselor; take a practice PSAT/NMSQT. https://studentaid.gov/resources/prepare-for-college/checklists/10th-grade. Paraphrase of checklist. Federal guidance. Verified.
4. Grade 11 fall: take the PSAT/NMSQT, the National Merit qualifier. https://studentaid.gov/resources/prepare-for-college/checklists/11th-grade. Quote: "You must take the test in 11th grade" for National Merit. Verified.
5. Grade 11 spring: use a free scholarship search; some deadlines fall as early as the summer between 11th and 12th grade. Same page. Verified.
6. Summer before grade 12: create your own StudentAid.gov account (a parent should not create it for you) and apply for scholarships to cut borrowing. Same page. Verified.
7. Grade 12: submit the FAFSA by the earliest aid deadline of your schools; finish last scholarship applications; keep grades up because "second-semester grades can affect scholarship eligibility." https://studentaid.gov/resources/prepare-for-college/checklists/12th-grade. Verified.
8. When to apply: research and apply in the summer between junior and senior year, but do not give up if you missed it. FSA, Finding and Applying for Scholarships, https://studentaid.gov/understand-aid/types/scholarships. Paraphrase. Federal guidance, undated. Verified.

## B. Documents to assemble
9. Ask counselors and teachers well before deadlines to send transcript and recommendation letters. FSA 12th grade checklist (above). Quote: "ask your counselor and teachers to submit the required documents." Verified.
10. FAFSA results may be requested: after filing you get a FAFSA Submission Summary with your Student Aid Index, which some providers ask for. NSPA, https://www.scholarshipproviders.org/page/scholarinfo. Association guidance, undated. Verified.
11. Activity list: see finding 1 (FSA grade 9). No official source found for a "resume" format or CSS Profile timing. Not verified.

## C. How many applications, how much time
12. Apply for many awards and track due dates in a notebook or spreadsheet. NSPA (same URL). Quote: "Apply for as many scholarships as possible." No number given. Association guidance. Verified.
13. Read the application carefully, fill it out completely, meet the deadline. FSA scholarships page. Verified.
14. Incomplete applications are not considered. UNCF, https://uncf.org/scholarships (provider's own advice, current). Quote: "Complete all steps for the applications!" Verified.
15. Three of four students who did not use a scholarship never applied. Sallie Mae / Ipsos, How America Pays for College, 19th edition, 2026, https://www.salliemae.com/about/leading-research/how-america-pays-for-college/. Research study: online survey, 1,000 undergraduates plus 1,000 parents, April 22 to May 26, 2026, weighted to 2023 CPS. Verified (summary page only; I did not read the full PDF).

## D. Local versus national (see Not supported)
16. NSPA says big national awards pay more but are harder to win, so spend more effort on local ones. NSPA (same URL). Quote: "Your chances of being selected tend to be much greater." Association opinion with no data. Verified, but it is an assertion, not evidence.

## E. Deadlines and specific programs
17. National Merit: take the PSAT/NMSQT no later than the third year of grades 9 to 12. PSAT 10 and PSAT 8/9 do not count. NMSC, Entry Requirements, https://www.nationalmerit.org/s/1758/interior.aspx?sid=1758&gid=2&pgid=1876. Provider's own rules, 2026 (for the 2028 competition). About 3.5 million take the test and about 1.2 million meet entry requirements. Verified.
18. Horatio Alger undergraduate: juniors apply Dec 1 to Mar 1, seniors Dec 1 to Feb 15; criteria are critical financial need, integrity and perseverance through adversity. https://horatioalger.org/scholarships/. Provider, current. Verified. The page gives no applicant tips.
19. UNCF: each program has its own eligibility and deadlines; check each listing. https://uncf.org/scholarships. Provider, current. Verified.
20. BigFuture Scholarships (College Board's own giveaway) uses monthly drawings, no essay or GPA, for completing planning steps starting in grade 10. Source: search result summary of https://bigfuture.collegeboard.org/pay-for-college/bigfuture-scholarships/official-rules-class-2027. Unverified (page not opened).
21. President's Volunteer Service Award: reported paused since May 27, 2025 by AmeriCorps; no resume date. https://presidentialserviceawards.gov/. Federal program. Unverified (site would not load). Do not tell students to earn it without a caveat.

## F. Avoiding scams
22. Never pay to apply; walk away from "processing cost" or "redemption fee." FTC, https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-avoid-scholarship-and-financial-aid-scams. Federal guidance, undated (current page). Quote: "Never pay to apply for a scholarship." Verified.
23. Warning phrases: guaranteed or money back; "you're a finalist" for a contest you never entered; card or bank number to "hold" an award. Same FTC page. Verified.
24. Legitimate companies "never guarantee or promise scholarships." Same FTC page. Verified.
25. The FAFSA is free; never share your FSA ID. FTC page above and FSA, https://studentaid.gov/resources/scams. Federal guidance. Verified.

## G. Outside scholarships and aid (displacement)
26. Report every outside scholarship to the college aid office; ask whether grants or loans are cut first. BigFuture, https://bigfuture.collegeboard.org/pay-for-college/grants-and-scholarships/how-outside-scholarships-affect-your-financial-aid-package. Association (College Board), undated. Quote: "the scholarship dollars replace financial-aid-package dollars." Verified.
27. Federal rule: total aid cannot exceed cost of attendance, so the school subtracts the scholarship. FSA scholarships page. Verified. BigFuture adds that a school must reduce need-based aid if total aid exceeds need by $300 or more (verified, same page).
28. NSPA warns that a private scholarship can reduce institutional aid and that spending it on room, board or travel may be taxable. NSPA page. Verified.
29. Prevalence: a 2013 survey of 61 four-year colleges found half reduced institutional aid when outside awards came in. New America, https://www.newamerica.org/insights/now-you-see-it-now-you-dont-scholarship-displacement-dilemma/, April 14, 2020. Verified that the article says this. The survey's author is not named there; a Hechinger summary reportedly says only about one-fifth acknowledged doing so. Treat the number as uncertain.
30. uAspire advises tracking amounts and asking providers to let funds cover full cost of attendance. https://www.uaspire.org/news-events/yes,-colleges-really-can-cut-your-financial-aid-if. Nonprofit, April 20, 2023. Verified. It cites "about half of students" affected but names no study.

## H. Statistics
31. 61% of students received a scholarship in 2025-26; average award $8,291. Sallie Mae / Ipsos, 19th edition, 2026 (n=1,000 students, 1,000 parents). Verified (summary page). This counts any scholarship, not only private ones.
32. Scholarships and grants covered 27% of spending in 2024-25 (from the 2025 edition, snippet only, unverified).

## I. Canada
33. Federal aid is applied for through your province or territory; apply at least two months before your program starts. canada.ca student aid pages. Federal guidance. Unverified (summary only). The main page I opened gave no deadlines.

## NOT SUPPORTED (no reputable source found)
- "Local scholarships are easier to win / have better odds." Only NSPA asserts it ("tend to"), with no data. The logic (fewer eligible applicants) is plausible but I found no win-rate study. Do not cite specific multipliers such as "10x"; they trace to advice blogs.
- "Apply to at least N scholarships" (10, 20, etc.). NSPA says "as many as possible" and gives no number. No source gives a number.
- "Spend X hours per week on scholarships." None found.
- "Reuse essays" and "ask for recommendation letters N weeks ahead." No official source found in the pages read; FSA only says "well before" deadlines.
- Any statistic for the percent of students getting PRIVATE scholarships specifically or typical private amounts. I did not locate the NCES NPSAS private-grant figures (searches returned only totals: 72% received some aid in 2019-20). Use the Sallie Mae figures labeled as "any scholarship."
- That the PVSA is a scholarship booster. Paused, and no source ties it to awards.
